==================================================================== 1.
PROJECT OVERVIEW
====================================================================

AI&DS Career Compass 3.0 is a student-first career-awareness and
project-based learning platform for AI and Data Science students.

Its purpose is to help students: - Understand their current skills,
interests and learning position. - Explore career roles that may suit
their profile. - Understand skills associated with those roles. -
Compare their skills with selected employer job requirements. - Explore
curated market signals, salary context and economic/technology shifts. -
Identify learning gaps and choose practical projects. - Track learning
tasks and continue working on them.

Core promise: Learn useful skills, understand their possible market
value, practise realistic problems, and build evidence of ability.

This is a guidance and learning prototype. It does not promise a job,
placement, salary, hiring outcome or guaranteed career success.

==================================================================== 2.
PROBLEM STATEMENT
====================================================================

Despite the availability of numerous learning resources, online
platforms, courses and career opportunities, many students struggle to
become employable and prepared for placements or jobs.

Students may not know: - Which skills are useful in real-world work. -
Which career paths exist and which may fit their interests. - What
skills employers expect. - How the job market and technology landscape
are changing. - How to connect classroom/theoretical learning with
practical work. - How to demonstrate their ability through projects and
evidence. - What they should learn or build next.

The central problem is the gap between access to learning resources and
practical career preparation. Having access to courses does not
automatically give students direction, practical experience, employer
awareness or proof of ability.

The problem has several connected parts:

1.  Career-direction confusion: students may not understand available
    roles or which path fits them.
2.  Skill-priority confusion: there are many languages, tools,
    frameworks and technologies, making it difficult to choose what to
    learn first.
3.  Theory-to-practice gap: understanding a topic does not necessarily
    mean being able to solve a practical problem.
4.  Unclear employer expectations: students may not distinguish required
    skills, preferred skills and skills they can genuinely demonstrate.
5.  Changing market conditions: technology, business, policy and
    economic shifts may change the value of skills and roles.
6.  Weak evidence of ability: completing courses alone may not
    demonstrate practical competence; students need projects, code,
    reports, charts, demos and documentation.
7.  Lack of a next-step plan: students may not know what to learn next
    or in what order.
8.  Misleading career/salary expectations: salary figures and market
    claims can be misunderstood as guaranteed outcomes.

The project focuses on this student employability and preparation
problem. It does not claim to have measured unemployment or placement
rates across colleges, tiers or all students. Any percentages or
comparisons discussed during brainstorming are not project findings
unless backed by a documented dataset and method.

==================================================================== 3.
PROPOSED SOLUTION AND HOW IT WORKS
====================================================================

The solution is a connected website/application workflow:

1.  Student enters a profile.
2.  The system compares the profile with a curated career catalogue.
3.  The system displays career recommendations and explains why each
    role may fit.
4.  The student reviews matched and missing skills.
5.  The student compares their skills with selected real job-description
    requirements.
6.  The student explores tracked skills, market signals, salary context
    and economic shifts.
7.  The student chooses a learning sequence or practical project in
    Learning Studio.
8.  The student adds project/learning tasks to My Progress.
9.  The student updates task status and can attach an evidence URL.
10. The student returns to continue learning and building evidence.

The platform aims to connect profile, role, skills, employer evidence,
market context, practical work and progress rather than simply listing
courses.

The career fit score is a profile-overlap indicator. It is not a hiring
probability, placement prediction or guarantee.

==================================================================== 5.
CAREER RECOMMENDATION ENGINE
====================================================================

Main logic: engine.py Career data: career_catalog.json

The engine: - Normalises skill names using aliases. - Compares profile
skills and interests with career requirements. - Calculates a fit
score. - Explains reasons for a match. - Lists matched and missing
skills. - Provides starter-project and learning guidance. - Uses year of
study and weekly learning hours for guidance.

Inspected scoring weights: - Skills overlap: 0.40. - Interests overlap:
0.40. - Preferred work-style overlap: 0.10 when supplied. - Target-goal
match: 0.10 when supplied. Optional weights are renormalised when
optional profile fields are absent.

The engine also contains job_skill_gap comparison logic, which
distinguishes matched skills from skills that are “not reported or not
assessed” and calculates a coverage percentage. A skill not mentioned in
a job description should not automatically be treated as a skill the
employer does not value.

Six roles are currently in career_catalog.json: 1. Data Analyst. 2. BI
Analyst. 3. Data Scientist. 4. Data Engineer. 5. ML Engineer. 6. Applied
AI / Generative AI Developer.

The first five are labelled established in the catalogue; Applied AI /
Generative AI Developer is labelled emerging.

Catalogue fields include role title/category, description, required
skills, interests, work types, starter project, learning sources,
job-market skill labels and caveats.

Limitations: - The catalogue is a career guide, not a verified universal
employer-requirements list. - Job-market skill labels are not posting
counts or measured frequency. - Scoring weights are not empirically
validated. - Skills are self-reported and not independently verified. -
Some collected profile fields are not used in scoring. - Goal matching
is relatively simple text matching. - Empty/missing inputs and other
scoring edge cases need review.

MARKET EXPLORER
====================================================================

File: pages/6_Market_Explorer.py Data: market_signals.json

Purpose: help students explore skills, roles, salary context and
economic/technology changes that may influence skill value.

Inspected features: - Search and filtering of tracked skills. - Skill
explanations and use cases. - Related roles and skills. - Signal
labels/scores. - Current signal descriptions. - Outlook through 2030. -
Sector/opportunity information. - Role-based salary context. -
Economic-shift explanations. - Source links and methodology/caveats.

Tracked skill areas include Python, SQL, statistics, data engineering,
machine learning, deep learning, NLP, MLOps, cloud and responsible AI.

The market dataset snapshot is dated 27 October 2026. Its 1–5 signal
scores are qualitative synthesis. They are not measured job-posting
counts, hiring probabilities or a universal demand index.

The project specification expects the page to distinguish: - Measured
observations. - Published research/reports. - Qualitative synthesis. -
Editorial hypotheses.

Economic-shift information should explain what changed or may change,
which roles/skills may be affected, why, whether it is
observed/reported/hypothetical, the source/date, and uncertainty or
alternative outcomes.

Salary context: - Salary is role-based, not the price or guaranteed
return of learning a skill. - Annual LPA ranges are the underlying
context. - Monthly, weekly and hourly figures may be arithmetic
conversions from annual gross salary. - Such conversions are not
necessarily take-home pay or advertised hourly rates. - Sources should
be checked for Indian fresher relevance, experience, location and
date. - Salary ranges are not promises.

Limitations: - Curated dated data, not live data. - 2030 outlook is
uncertain and must not be presented as fact. - Sources need a final
verification pass. - Qualitative scores must not be plotted or described
as vacancy counts. - Any future measured-demand chart needs a repeatable
method, date, source, query/selection rules, role, location, experience
level, sample size and deduplication method.

DATABASE AND DATA PROTECTION
====================================================================

File: database.py Database: data/career_compass.db

Inspected SQLite tables:

students: - Student ID, created timestamp, display name, year of study,
profile JSON.

assessments: - Student ID foreign key, created timestamp,
recommendations JSON, action-plan JSON.

progress_tasks: - Student ID foreign key, assessment ID foreign key,
task title, detail, evidence URL, status, due week, updated timestamp.

job_sources: - Job ID, employer, role, location, source URL, checked
date, payload JSON.

Important preservation note: The current project folder contains
data/career_compass.db. Earlier continuity documentation said a database
from the user’s earlier laptop/archive was not included in the available
archive. Do not assume the current database is empty or overwrite it.
Back it up before schema changes, migration or replacement. Keep the
older project folder untouched as a fallback until the new version is
verified.

Database fix completed: The original connection helper returned a raw
SQLite connection and did not reliably close it when used through a
context manager. This caused Windows WinError32 temporary database
file-lock errors in tests.

database.py was changed to use a connection context manager that: -
Creates the parent directory if needed. - Opens the SQLite connection. -
Sets row_factory. - Enables foreign keys. - Yields the connection. -
Commits on success. - Rolls back on failure. - Closes the connection in
a finally block.

This fixed the observed test file-lock errors.

Data safety rules: - Never reset or silently migrate the database. -
Back up before schema changes. - Tests must use temporary databases. -
Tests must not modify the user’s actual database. - Do not package
personal learner data, secrets or API keys in a public ZIP/repository.

be committed or included in a public package.

25. RELEASE-HARDENING UPDATE — 1 NOVEMBER 2026
======================================================================

This section records the code and documentation changes made after the original project report. It supersedes the original test count and implementation status where the two differ.

Source package used:
CareerCompass3_Sequence_Update.zip

The supplied source ZIP contained the application source files and tests, but did NOT contain data/career_compass.db. Work was performed in a separate copy extracted from that ZIP. No database from the user's computer was copied, replaced, reset, migrated, or opened. The release ZIP produced from this work intentionally excludes local SQLite databases and personal learner records.

25.1 Database connection safety

- database.py now provides a context-managed SQLite connection.
- Every connection is closed in a finally block.
- Successful operations commit; exceptions roll back and propagate.
- Tables continue to use CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS.
- No destructive schema migration or database reset was added.
- A new latest_assessment(student_id) function reads the latest saved assessment for an explicitly supplied student ID; it does not select a student automatically.

25.2 Student record selection / privacy guard

- My Progress no longer chooses the newest student record when no active profile exists.
- Learning Studio no longer chooses the newest student record when no active profile exists.
- Both pages require an explicitly active profile in the current browser session to save or view progress.
- The recent-record listing was removed from My Progress to avoid casually displaying other local record names/details on a shared machine.
- UI text explains that local session state is not authentication and that this prototype is not secure multi-user software.

Important limitation: this is a guard against accidental automatic record selection, not a login system. The app does not provide authenticated student isolation.

25.3 Evidence URL validation

- Added validate_evidence_url() in engine.py.
- Blank optional evidence links remain allowed.
- Nonblank evidence links must be complete HTTP or HTTPS URLs.
- The validator rejects other schemes, absent hosts, whitespace/control characters, malformed ports, embedded usernames/passwords, and links longer than 2,048 characters.
- My Progress checks the URL before storing it.
- UI warns that external links are not independently verified by Career Compass.

25.4 Recommendation transparency card

- Each recommendation now contains score_breakdown details for Skills, Interests, and any optional Preferred Work Style or Career Goal component.
- Each component reports overlap percentage, effective weight percentage, and contribution points.
- Effective displayed weights are normalised to 100 percent.
- The original scoring formula and original weight assignments were retained.
- The Results page displays a “Why this fit score?” expander.
- Low-information profiles are marked provisional instead of making the fit score appear more conclusive than the inputs support.

25.5 Use of additional profile fields

- Python level, favourite subjects, and described project experience now affect guidance text.
- Those fields do not change the numerical fit score.
- Project descriptions are treated as self-reported context, not as independently verified skill evidence.
- Results UI labels selected skills as self-reported and explains which profile fields do not affect the score.

25.6 Saved assessment reload

- Results page has a “Reload saved assessment for this active profile” action.
- It reloads the latest assessment only for student_id already present in the current Streamlit session.
- It does not search for another student or automatically pick the latest record in the database.
- If the browser session has fully reset and student_id is lost, the current prototype cannot recover the student's identity automatically. This limitation is deliberate until a proper identity/access model exists.

25.7 Market, employer, and learning evidence clarity

- The market hero text now describes a dated snapshot instead of “today's market signals”.

- My Progress explains that status/evidence links are learner-reported and do not independently verify mastery.

25.11 Database protection when installing

The release ZIP has no data/career_compass.db. If applying these source updates to an existing project folder on Windows, first close Streamlit, back up the existing `data/career_compass.db`, and keep the old project folder untouched as fallback. Extract into a new folder for the first test. Do not copy/migrate/replace the existing database automatically.
